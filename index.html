01 — About
About Me

Hi, I'm Saud Alotaibi. This website is for my Corpus Linguistics course project. It explores how large collections of real language data can reveal patterns, structure, and meaning in human communication.

Through this course, we examine authentic texts using computational tools and linguistic theory to uncover how language actually works in the real world — not just how we think it should work.

"A corpus gives us access to language as it is actually used — not as grammarians imagine it to be."
— Douglas Biber, Corpus Linguistics
What is this course?

This course introduces the field of corpus linguistics — the study of language through large, structured collections of real texts known as corpora. Students learn to use specialist tools to analyse frequency, collocation, concordance, and register variation across different text types and genres.

03 — Lessons
Core Lessons
Lesson 01

What is a Corpus?

A corpus is a large, structured collection of authentic texts compiled for linguistic analysis. It can be written, spoken, or multimodal, and is designed to represent a particular variety or domain of language use.

Lesson 02

Concordance Lines

A concordance displays every occurrence of a search word in its surrounding context (KWIC: Key Word In Context). Concordance lines allow researchers to observe patterns of use that would be impossible to detect by reading alone.

Lesson 03

Collocation

Collocation refers to the tendency of certain words to regularly co-occur. For example, heavy collocates strongly with rain and traffic. Corpus tools measure collocation using statistical measures like MI (Mutual Information) and T-score.

Lesson 04

Frequency Lists & Keyness

Frequency lists rank words by how often they appear in a corpus. Keyness analysis compares two corpora to identify which words are statistically more frequent — or "key" — in one text versus a reference corpus.

Lesson 05

Register Variation

Different situations of language use — academic writing, conversation, news — show systematic linguistic differences. Corpus linguistics allows us to quantify these differences across vocabulary, grammar, and discourse features.
